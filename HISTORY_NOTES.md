# Historical basis for the July 1914 scene

The July 1914 world is a teaching model, not a claim that five marks can fully
explain the First World War. It deliberately begins with an inadequate
`assassination → war` arrow so the human and agent can challenge it together.

## What the visible dates mean

- **28 June:** Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated at Sarajevo.
- **23 July:** Austria-Hungary delivered its ultimatum to Serbia.
- **30 July:** Russia ordered general mobilization.
- **1–3 August:** Germany declared war on Russia and France.
- **4 August:** Britain declared war on Germany after the invasion of Belgium.

The event sequence is grounded in the [Imperial War Museums' July Crisis
factsheet](https://www.iwm.org.uk/sites/default/files/press-release/4_August_1914_Factsheet.pdf),
the U.S. Office of the Historian's [1914 diplomatic papers by
country](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914Supp/toc-countries),
and the peer-reviewed [1914–1918 Online account of the July
Crisis](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/july-crisis-1914/?version=1.0).

## Why the source fragments disagree productively

The German assurance to Austria-Hungary, often called the **blank cheque**,
supports an interpretation in which alliance commitments enabled escalation.
The later Willy–Nicky telegrams show the German and Russian emperors still
appealing to one another personally, complicating any claim that the outcome
was mechanically inevitable. The point is not that either fragment settles the
question; it is that a causal model should expose evidence and tension instead
of presenting one confident arrow. See the [blank cheque source
discussion](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/germanys-blank-cheque-to-austria-hungary/)
and [July Crisis historiography](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/july-crisis-1914-1-1/).

## Counterfactual guardrail

Crossing out Russian mobilization does **not** make TRACE assert that war would
have been prevented. It weakens the immediate path to the German declarations,
draws a possible negotiation window, and keeps the label “a reopened path, not
a prediction.” This is a question-generating counterfactual: it shows which
dependencies change and which other pressures remain.

For a production educational tool, each event and relationship would carry
source-level citations, perspective metadata, confidence, and alternative
interpretations. The prototype demonstrates the interaction and epistemic
guardrails in one compact scene.
